The parts in your kit were rescued from old electronics collected at Nehru Place, Delhi. Follow their trip, one stop at a time.

The trip of your kit

Scroll down the line. Each stop is a place your parts have been.

  1. Stop 1

    Picked up at Nehru Place

    Nehru Place, New Delhi

    We collected old and broken electronics from Nehru Place, one of the biggest computer and electronics markets in Asia. These were mice, cables, boards and gadgets that were heading for the bin.

  2. Stop 2

    Sorted by type

    Everything was laid out and split into piles: mice, cables, circuit boards, motors, buttons, gears and screws. Each pile became a possible part of your kit.

  3. Stop 3

    Safety screening

    Before any part could join a kit, it was checked. Anything cracked, sharp, damaged or unsafe for young hands was taken out and sent for recycling instead.

  4. Stop 4

    Chosen for REWIRE

    The parts that passed were cleaned and picked for what they can teach: gears to turn, motors to spin, resistors to wire up and buttons to press.

  5. Stop 5

    Packed into your kit

    A mix of these parts was packed with an activity guide and sent to you. No two kits hold exactly the same old device parts.

  6. Left over

    Responsible recycling

    Whatever could not be reused was sent to responsible e-waste recycling, so it does not end up in a landfill or get burned.

  7. You are here

    Your workbench

    Open the box. The next stop is whatever you decide to build.

A second life

The parts you hold once lived inside something people used every day. When you build with them, they stay in use instead of becoming waste.

Keeping things in use for as long as possible is called the circular economy, and now you are part of it.
